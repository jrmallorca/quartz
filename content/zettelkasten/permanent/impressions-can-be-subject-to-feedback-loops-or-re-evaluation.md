# Impressions can be subject to feedback loops or re-evaluation

The [[halo-effect]] creates related or unrelated impressions about other characteristics, which are subject to being either further reinforced or re-evaluated depending on a result. The feedback loop requires both an initial impression and an expected result from trusting that impression to further reinforce it. As it requires 2 ingredients, the feedback loop isn't autocatalytic.

**Example 1**

- A person has a good "halo" from being in a team that succeeds a lot of their projects, even if the person did not contribute much.
- The person has been granted an opportunity to work in a bigger project with a new team.
- If the new team succeeds with the new person, then the "halo" may be further reinforced even if the person did not contribute much again.
- Alternatively, if the new team fails or the new team complains about the person, then their halo will be subject to re-evaluation.

**Example 2**

- A product creates an impression of quality due to its popularity, attractive packaging, and price.
- On use, the product gives an expected result of being a high quality. The impression of quality has been reinforced.
- Alternatively, the product does not perform as expected which subjects the impression of quality to re-evaluation.
