# Behaviour is likely to be repeated when the experience is satisfying and vice versa

In [[dopamine-is-the-reward-prediction-error-in-reinforcement-learning-in-animals]], we observe that [[dopamine]] spikes during the [[reward-in-terms-of-habits]] if an experience is either immediately satisfying or unsatisfying. Respectively, these effects are due to either a [[positive-reward-prediction-error]] or [[negative-reward-prediction-error]].

As [[dopamine-is-related-to-motivation]], behaviour is likely to be repeated when the experience is immediately satisfying and vice versa. [[habit-rewards-teach-worthwhile-actions-for-the-future]].

Consider the following examples:
- The feeling of pleasure during sex versus the reward of offspring.
- Using premium soaps to feel high class versus reducing the risk of diarrhea.
- Pain from exercise versus the reward of physical prowess.
