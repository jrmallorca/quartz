# Extracting underlying principles is learned through interleaved and varied practice

The skill to [[extract-underlying-principles]] behind anything is better learned through [[interleaved-practice]] and [[varied-practice]] than [[massed-practice]] as [[interleaved-practice-emphasises-the-differences-between-each-topic]], similar to [[varied-practice]].

## Concrete example

An example from the book "Make It Stick":

> Interleaving the identification of bird types or the works of oil painters improves your ability both to learn the unifying attributes within a type and to differentiate between types, improving your skill at categorizing new specimens you encounter later

Personally, [[varied-practice]] would give the simplest example. Consider the following expressions:

`x + 5 = 10`
`x + 3 = 10`
`x + 5 = 7`
`x + 3 = 7`

With these expressions, we're practising the same problem but with different factors (the `x`). Once we practised this, we can extrapolate this to other expressions or problems that are similar to what we practised (e.g., `y + 8 = 6`).
