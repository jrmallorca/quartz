# Delayed feedback is more effective than immediate feedback

While [[feedback-strengthens-retention]], delayed feedback is much more effective than immediate feedback. This is consistent with how humans learn motor tasks.

With immediate feedback, the learner eventually comes to depend upon the continued presence of the correction.

## Analogy

## Concrete example

### Immediate feedback

- For motor skills, this would be similar to training wheels on a bike.
- In terms of studying, this would be like an open-book exam where students can correct their answers by re-reading the material for the test.

### Delayed feedback

- As delayed feedback is how we learn our motor skills, then this would be like learning to shoot a basketball into the hoop.
- In terms of studying, this would be like a traditional test where you attempt to answer everything and get the feedback after the test is finished.
