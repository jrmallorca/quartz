# Desirable difficulty in learning

Desirable difficulty in learning is a short-term impediment that introduces [[goldilocks-difficulty-for-motivation]] by simultaneously slowing down and creating more effort for the learning process.

Consider the following examples:

- [[interleaved-practice-creates-desirable-difficulties-for-learning-via-spacing-of-topics]]
- [[varied-practice]]

[[desirable-difficulties-in-learning-improve-learning]]
▪ difficulties that elicit more effort and that slow down learning—spacing, interleaving, mixing up practice, and others—will more than compensate for their inconvenience by making the learning stronger, more precise, and more enduring. Short-term impediments that make for stronger learning have come to be called desirable difficulties, a term coined by the psychologists Elizabeth and Robert Bjork.2
