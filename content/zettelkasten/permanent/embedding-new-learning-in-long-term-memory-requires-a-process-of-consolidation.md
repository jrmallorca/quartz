# Embedding new learning in long-term memory requires a process of consolidation

[[spaced-repetition]] is more effective than [[massed-practice]] because embedding new learning in long term memory requires a process of consolidation: memory traces (the brain's representation of new learning) are strengthened, given meaning, and connected to prior knowledge.

Consolidation can take a matter of hours to several days and massed practice does not allow for retriggering consolidation. This is because [[massed-practice-results-in-the-worst-information-retention-rate]] due to a lack of [[desirable-difficulty]].

## Analogy

Imagine composing an essay. At first, it is rough and imprecise. We reflect to see what we can add, improve, and/or delete for the next revision. Through the repetition of reflecting and writing, the essay has become a bit more refined. We take a break.

After a break, perhaps a day or a week, we return and see more improvements that can be made upon the essay. Maybe we finally understand what the main messages we want to deliver are. Through it, we draw upon our previous knowledge, connect them to our main messages, and rearrange the structure of the essay to more effectively deliver the essay to be more eloquent and elegant.
