# Disadvantages of Help to Buy - Wales equity loan

The following information has been gathered from [this youtube link](https://yewtu.be/watch?v=uIZwdKijL4w) which explains Help to Buy for England.

The example used for the points below will be the following:

- On a £300,000 property
- 20% loan (£60,000).
- 5% deposit (£15,000).
- Property has increased to £400,000.

- You don't owe the government the original up to 20% borrowed, you owe them 20% of the CURRENT MARKET VALUE of the property.
  - Source: [[staircasing-help-to-buy-wales-equity-loan]]
  - You now owe 20% of £400,000 which is £80,000.
- Each repayment must be at least 10% of the current market value of the property.
  - Source: [[staircasing-help-to-buy-wales-equity-loan]]
  - You now pay 10% of £400,000, which is £40,000, in lump sum. If you took a 20% loan, you must pay another 10% of the current market value which will likely be higher than £40,000.
- With the [[help-to-buy-wales-equity-loan-interest-rate-increase-calculation]], the estimation of the interest rate in at least 5 years time will be difficult.
  - With the [current CPI from December 2022](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/december2022), assuming we use 10.5% as the CPI and the interest rate as the initial 1.75%,
    - New interest rate =
    - 100(0.0175 + (0.0175(0.105 + 0.02)) =
    - 100(0.0175 + (0.0021875)) =
    - 100(0.0196875) =
    - 1.97% (2 d.p.)
  - While the above is still good, interest compounds. It may quickly become uncontrollable the bigger it is.
- Cannot let out property.
