# Habit formation naturally optimises behaviour

Due to the [[feedback-loop-of-all-human-behaviour]], [[habit]] formation will naturally undergo [[reinforcement-learning]] to optimise behaviour to be more efficient. It aligns with [[neuroplasticity]], as [[habit-formation-minimises-cognitive-load-usage-for-other-activities]], or the [[said-principle]].

Remember this quote from Bruce Lee - "Absorb what is useful, discard what is useless and add what is specifically your own".

With a novel behaviour, [[intensity-of-cognition-is-directly-correlated-to-novelty-of-stimuli]]. We will be trying everything to achieve the goal. If we fail, we learn what not to do; if we succeed, we learn the behaviours required to succeed. Eventually we follow Bruce Lee's saying of absorbing the useful, discarding the useless, and adding upgrades.
