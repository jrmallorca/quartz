# Choosing mental models requires extraction of underlying principles

Each [[mental-model]] provides a [[cue-in-terms-of-habits]] that indicates it is applicable for a piece of information. To identify the cue from the complex pieces of information from reality, the ability to [[extract-underlying-principles]] is required.

## Example 1

Take a company that wants to make more profit.

Immediately we know of the mental model that profit is revenue after all expenses. Therefore we could go 2 ways:

1. Increase revenue. We could think of the mental model of the link between supply-demand and opt to increase demand or decrease supply. A decrease in supply indicates the mental model of the scarcity effect.
2. Decrease expenses. We may encounter a new piece of information that they're hosting their systems on-premises. This allows us to suggest hosting on the cloud as it would allow them to be more scalable for a cheaper price.

## Example 2

The application of mental models can be more general and autonomous.

Take the scene of a beach.

- A biologist may look towards birds in the distance and general wildlife.
- A zoologist may have more of a focus on the birds instead.
- A real-estate developer may attempt to understand the value of the land.
- A person harmed or stolen from may not even notice the beauty and surveil for dangerous people.
