# The greatest threat to success is boredom with our habits

As phrased in Atomic Habits:

> The greatest threat to success is not failure but boredom. We get bored with habits because they stop delighting us. The outcome becomes expected. And as our habits become ordinary, we start derailing our progress to seek novelty.

Boredom with habits, even when they're giving satisfactory or good results, will cause us to seek novelty. This novelty will cause us to either:

- change the process for the better (e.g., [[organise-standardise-optimise-for-habit-formation]]),
- for worse (e.g., less frequently achieved),
- or even given up entirely.

Boredom may be caused by a combination of the following:

- Minimised cognitive load or higher automaticity as [[habit-formation-minimises-cognitive-load-usage-for-other-activities]] and [[intensity-of-cognition-is-directly-correlated-to-novelty-of-stimuli]].
- Demotivation from the habit as it becomes an [[lpb-in-premacks-principle]].
- Lack of visible and/or tangible results. [[good-habits-are-easy-to-break-as-results-are-either-slow-or-intangible]].

This is why [[bad-habits-are-engineered-to-be-superstimuli-from-corporations]] as they provide continuous forms of novelty to never get bored of the (bad) habit.
