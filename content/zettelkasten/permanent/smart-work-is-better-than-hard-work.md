# Smart work is better than hard work

Smart work is much better to operate with than hard work. It minimises physical or mental strain that can be used for other purposes.

However, it doesn't mean to completely avoid hard work. Hard work is often the [[standardisation-before-optimisation]], where optimisation is smart work, or is used in the beginning for a [[zettelkasten/permanent/habit]] until we learn to optimise.

## Concrete example

Imagine that we have a goal of performing a [[pull-up]]. We could attempt 100 pull-ups everyday and eventually we may progress. Alternatively, we could use [[progressive-overload]] and attempt a variety of pulling exercises: rows, pull-up negatives, etc. While there is still an element of hard work involved, the smart work of planning will optimise our time and energy expenditure to achieve a pull-up.

Another example is studying; most of us use [[massed-practice]] when there are other researched learning techniques that are more efficient: [[how-to-learn]].
