## Introduction

How do you learn right now? Are you studying a book or video over and over again without rest until you feel that you know it? This is the most common way you'll see people study. It is also the worst way to study.

Massed practice: the act of studying or rehearsing for something without rest in a learning session. It can look like:

- constantly rereading the same pages in a book,
- or trying to get really good on 1 skill before moving onto to the next.

While it might feel good for the moment, you're actually falling for the "illusion of mastery". You've familiarised yourself with the material but have not understood what it means. For example, you may have memorised the definition of a complicated word, but you aren't able to use it in a sentence.

Instead, we will combine effective learning techniques using 2 tools. These learning techniques are:

- Extracting underlying principles
- Spaced repetition
- Interleaved practice
- Varied practice
- Elaboration
- Generation
- Reflection
- Calibration
- Mnemonics
- Rest

Don't worry about the meaning behind them or why they work for now; it will be explained in the [theory behind efficient learning](#theory-behind-efficient-learning). Just know that the majority of these learning techniques will be combined in these 4 applications:

- [Zettelkasten](#zettelkasten)
- [Flashcards](#flashcards)
- [Habits](#habits)
- [Rest](#rest)

For each of these 4 applications, I will provide 2 types of examples to show that they can be applied to any topic. These examples will relate to either:

- Division
- Tucking in your shirt or top
